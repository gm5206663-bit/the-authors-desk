# THE COMPLETE GUIDE
## How to write gated, canon-true fan fiction — for any fandom

**The house method, collected and organised. 2026-10-11.**

# PART 1 · THE FIVE DECISIONS

Do not write a word until all five are answered and written down.

## 1.1 Which era?

Pick one and stay in it. Every fandom has eras, and each era has different rules, different characters, and different documented facts.

**The trap:** freedom reads as licence. The eras with the least canon are the most dangerous, not the safest. A pre-canon story still needs every lock.

## 1.2 Who is your protagonist — and what are they *not*?

Write down what your character is **not** as carefully as what they are.

**The commonest failure in all of fan fiction:** an original character who quietly acquires a bloodline, a system, a past life, or a canon character's private mechanic. It happens one small permission at a time.

Decide the ceiling now. If your character has a public identity and a private one, decide which is which and **what the reveal costs**. If you don't, the reveal will happen by accident in chapter forty.

## 1.3 Where does your story touch canon?

**Name the canon beats your story will actually reach. Not "the world" — specific events.**

If you cannot name five, your story is not set in that world. It is set *next to* it.

> A serial once ran **fifteen chapters and 33,000 words without a single canon character or event appearing.** It was set in a famous location and never once mentioned the thing that location exists for. The cure cost one sentence.

## 1.4 What is the spine — who is hunting your protagonist?

Write one sentence, with all three blanks filled:

> **"\_\_\_\_\_\_ wants \_\_\_\_\_\_ from my protagonist, and will \_\_\_\_\_\_ to get it."**

If you cannot fill all three blanks, **stop and fix that first.** You do not have a story yet. You have a setting.

> A serial shipped **90,000 words without this sentence.** The entire rebuild was filling it in. One sentence, and suddenly fifteen chapters had a spine.

## 1.5 What will you never do?

Every project needs a short list of absolutes — things that are true regardless of what the scene wants.

Examples the house has used: *the protagonist never kills* · *no identity reveal without approval* · *a canon character's fate is never rerouted* · *power is never reduced to protect an outline.*

Write yours down **before you need them**, because the moment you need them is the moment you will be tempted.

> **One caution, learned the hard way:** an absolute you invent to close one gap becomes a wall you have to break later. *"Nothing is absolute"* — write them as **hard defaults**, and know which ones the author can overrule.

# PART 2 · THE KINDS OF FAN FICTION

Twelve shapes. Each one is a real, working form — and each carries its own trap.

**Choosing the shape is the first structural decision.** Most failures in this house were not writing failures; they were **shape** failures — a story written in a shape that could not carry it.

---

## 2.1 CANON-PARALLEL OC
*Your original character lives beside the canon protagonist. Canon runs on the page, in order, unskipped. Your character's life runs on the same clock.*

**The promise:** the reader gets the canon they came for, plus a new thread they did not expect.

**The rules**
- Canon runs **complete and unskipped.** You may not summarise a canon arc to get to your character
- Your character's scenes and canon's scenes run on the **same clock** — same years, same places, same events
- The two tracks touch **only where contact is natural.** The butterfly fires on contact, not on wish
- Canon's private scenes stay private. The reader may see them in a parallel panel; your character does not learn from them

**The trap:** your character becomes a passenger watching canon happen. **Test:** count your character's verbs. If they only *watch*, *file* and *note*, the chapter is broken.

**Best for:** readers who love the source and want more of it.

---

## 2.2 REINCARNATION / TRANSMIGRATION
*A person from elsewhere wakes up as someone in the story world.*

**The promise:** knowledge the world does not have, in a body that has to live with the consequences.

**The rules**
- **Decide what the memories are worth.** Do they know the plot? Do they know it accurately? Can it be changed? Answer all three before chapter one
- **The body's history is real.** The person they woke up as had a life, relationships, debts, and enemies. Those do not vanish because a new mind arrived
- **The canon knowledge is not a cheat code — it is a liability.** People notice when someone knows too much
- **Decide the reveal.** Who learns the truth, when, and what it costs

**The trap:** foreknowledge turns the story into a checklist of things the protagonist prevents. **The cure:** canon knowledge should create *new* problems as often as it solves old ones.

**Best for:** a protagonist who must act carefully because the world is dangerous to them specifically.

---

## 2.3 PRE-CANON / ERA-ORIGIN
*The story happens before the source story begins. You are building the world the canon will inherit.*

**The promise:** the origin of things the reader already knows.

**The rules**
- **Every element must eventually connect to canon**, or it is not pre-canon — it is a different story in the same setting
- **You are writing the causes of canon events.** Name them. If nothing you write causes anything in canon, you have chosen the wrong era
- **The world is not empty.** History is happening. Other people have plans
- **Restraint is the whole craft.** The reader knows how it ends. Your job is to make the *how* matter

**The trap:** writing a story that is *about* a character the reader already knows, with no causal link forward. **Test:** for each major event, name the canon thing it causes.

**Best for:** writers who love the world more than the plot.

---

## 2.4 DUAL-TRACK
*Two timelines or two viewpoints run in parallel and interleave.*

**The promise:** the reader holds two floors of the same building at once.

**The rules**
- **Mark the track clearly, every time.** A track change the reader does not notice is a continuity error they feel before they can name it
- **Both tracks must move.** A track that only reacts is decoration
- **The tracks must eventually pay each other off.** If they never touch, you have written two stories and stapled them
- **Same clock, same streets.** If both tracks share a world, they share its facts exactly

**The trap:** one track is the "real" story and the other becomes a commentary. **Test:** if you deleted one track, would the other lose something irreplaceable?

**Best for:** showing canon's own story *and* an original story at full weight.

---

## 2.5 THE SYSTEM / CHEAT PROTAGONIST
*Your character has a mechanism — a system, a ledger, a voice, a power that counts or measures or grants.*

**The promise:** visible progression the reader can track.

**The rules**
- **Name it, and let the owner name it.** A system the author names reads as an author's tool. A system the *character* names reads as theirs
- **The system must have rules it cannot break.** An all-purpose solution is not a system; it is a wish
- **Every gain answers five questions:** what changed · what caused it · what did they already possess that made this possible · **what failed first** · what limitation remains
- **The system is not sentient unless you decide it is** — and if it is, that is a character, with its own wants
- **The system must cost something.** Time, safety, exposure, or moral weight

**The trap:** the system becomes the protagonist, and the character becomes its operator. **Test:** strip the system out. Can the character still win anything? If not, you have written a system, not a person.

**Best for:** readers who love visible growth and honest numbers.

---

## 2.6 ADAPTATION / REBUILD
*A character or a story is re-made — reimagined, re-powered, given a different origin.*

**The promise:** a familiar thing, made new, with the original's bones intact.

**The rules**
- **Decide what is preserved and what changes, and write the list down.** Vague intent produces drift
- **The original's core must survive.** If the adaptation can do everything the original could plus more, you have not adapted it — you have replaced it
- **Changes propagate.** A different origin produces a different life, different skills, different relationships. Derive them
- **Label your inventions.** Everything you add is `[design]`, not canon

**The trap:** the adaptation is stronger, cooler, and less interesting than the original. **Test:** name the original's two best weaknesses. Does the adaptation still have them?

**Best for:** writers who want to explore a character the source under-used.

---

## 2.7 AU / DIVERGENCE
*One thing changes, and everything downstream changes with it.*

**The promise:** the consequences of a single difference, followed honestly.

**The rules**
- **One change, derived completely.** The value is in the derivation, not the change
- **Every divergence propagates:** trigger → immediate effect → secondary effect → character reaction → institutional reaction → long-term consequence
- **Never force canon back.** If your change makes the canon outcome impossible, the canon outcome is gone. Derive what replaces it
- **Never force a divergence either.** Not everything has to change. Let untouched things stay untouched
- **Mark the point of divergence precisely** — the chapter, the scene, the sentence

**The trap:** the change happens, and then the story carefully restores everything canon did anyway. **Test:** name three things that are now impossible because of your change.

**Best for:** writers who want to argue with the source.

---

## 2.8 NON-HUMAN POV
*The protagonist is not a person — a beast, a creature, a thing, a place.*

**The promise:** a perspective no human story can give.

**The rules**
- **The audience is still the protagonist.** The story is the beast's, not the people's. The commonest failure is letting human characters slowly take over the plot
- **The protagonist must act.** A creature that observes humans is a camera with fur
- **Translate without betraying.** The creature's understanding is not human, but the reader must follow it
- **Give it a clock.** Growth, seasons, ages, migrations — something must move

**The trap, recorded in this house:** chapters 15–18 of one serial measured **46–87% human characters**, and the humans carried the plot while the beast carried the audience. The author's words: *"they are nothing, there are nothing to do, oc is soul' beast not human… whom stroy we writeing."* All four chapters were rebuilt to **zero**.

**Best for:** writers who want to make the reader feel alien.

---

## 2.9 CROSSOVER
*Two or more sources in one story.*

**The promise:** the collision the reader never got.

**The rules**
- **Decide which source is the spine.** One world's rules govern. The other arrives into it
- **Rules do not blend.** One system wins, or you have to derive the interaction explicitly
- **Both casts must keep their own voices.** A crossover where everyone sounds the same is a costume party
- **The collision must change both sides.** If one world is unaffected, you have written an insert

**The trap:** power-scaling arguments replace story. **Test:** name what each world loses because the other arrived.

**Best for:** readers who know both sources and want the meeting.

---

## 2.10 FIX-IT AND NO-FIX
*The story either repairs a canon tragedy, or explicitly refuses to.*

**The rules for FIX-IT**
- **The fix must be earned.** It needs a cause, a cost, and a consequence
- **Fixing one thing breaks another.** Derive the damage
- **The fixer must be present for it.** No off-page rescues

**The rules for NO-FIX**
- **State the law plainly and keep it.** The house has a **No-Fix Law**: the story may not repair what canon broke
- **The tragedy must still be written well.** Refusing to fix something is not refusing to write it
- **Your character may grieve, rage, and fail to prevent it.** That is not a fix. That is a person

**The trap either way:** the reader can feel the author's hand. **Test:** could the opposite choice have happened, given the facts?

---

## 2.11 SERIAL vs BOOK vs ONE-SHOT
*The unit of publication is a structural choice with real consequences.*

| Form | Chapter band | What it demands |
|---|---|---|
| **One-shot** | 2,000–8,000 words | One turn, one register shift, one landed ending |
| **Book** | 20–60 chapters | An arc that resolves. The book must be able to end |
| **Serial** | Open-ended | Every chapter must work alone *and* in sequence. Ends are edges, not conclusions |
| **Arc-serial** | Serial made of 6–12 chapter arcs | The practical middle. Each arc has a shape; the serial continues |

**The rule for serials, learned at cost:** an open-ended serial still needs **a live edge** — one chapter that counts as the last one. Everything after is provisional.

---

## 2.12 THE FANDOM-AGNOSTIC METHOD
*How to apply all of the above to a fandom this house has never written.*

**Step 1 — Build a canon spine.** Read or watch the source. Write down the world's rules, the timeline's anchors, and the specific beats you will touch. **Tag every fact by confidence:** `[canon]` verified · `[fan]` plausible but unverified · `[disputed]` sources disagree · `[design]` your invention.

**Step 2 — Set the twelve locks** (Part 9, Card 1).

**Step 3 — Build the clock.** Every world has a way of measuring time. Find it, pick an anchor year, and derive every date from it.

**Step 4 — Find the progression system.** Every fandom has one, even when it is not called that. Find what *moves* — rank, skill, status, wealth, knowledge, relationship — and make it visible.

**Step 5 — Write the panel endpoints before the dates** (Part 6.2).

**Step 6 — Build the gates.** Start from a minimal checker. The house ships a working example (`examples/minigate.py`) and a CI recipe.

**Step 7 — Write chapter one.** Open in scene. Give it a threat and a face.

> **The rule that transfers to every fandom:** the method is the same everywhere. What changes is the spine, the clock, and the progression system. **The discipline does not change at all.**

# PART 3 · THE CRAFT

## 3.1 The five failures — *the diagnostic*

When a reader says "nonsense," do not re-word it. **Find what it skipped.** Walk these five in order.

| Failure | Symptom | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| **No canon spine** | Nothing from the source world actually happens on the page | Name the canon beats you touch. Five minimum |
| **Nothing contested** | No antagonist, no rival, no test. The protagonist's only verb is "keep" | Every chapter gets a threat and a face |
| **No felt progression** | Ranks, skills and status never move | Progression gates are structure. Use them |
| **One register repeated** | Every chapter reads the same | Five registers, at least three per chapter |
| **Apparatus outgrew the story** | Panels and ledgers longer than scenes | Slim panel. Bookkeeping lives outside the story |

> **If more than two are true, you are not editing — you are rebuilding.** Say so plainly and do it. Polishing a foundation-less serial wastes everyone's time and produces the same verdict twice.

## 3.2 The per-chapter contract

Every chapter must satisfy all seven. **Check them before drafting, not after.**

```
1. CANON BEAT   — what canon does this touch, and how does it stay true?
2. THREAT       — what can hurt someone here?
3. FACE         — who opposes, named, with dialogue?
4. MOVEMENT     — what changes: rank, skill, bond, resource, knowledge?
5. BUTTERFLY    — what does the protagonist's existence change about canon?
6. REGISTERS    — at least 3 of the five
7. TURN         — what is true at the end that was not true at the start?
```

> **A chapter that is only interior meditation fails and does not ship.** That is a hard rule, not a guideline.

## 3.3 The five registers

Fifteen chapters in one register is unreadable, however good each chapter is. **Use at least three per chapter.**

| Register | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Close sense** | Immediate physical experience — inside a body or a place |
| **Hard scene** | Real-time action with consequence — a fight, a hunt, an escape, a measurement |
| **Dialogue** | Spoken exchange between named characters |
| **Other POV** | A scene from someone else's eyes |
| **Lyrical** | Elevated, reflective, time-compressed — the long view |

> **The dialogue register is the one that goes missing.** Interior protagonists do not talk, and introspective prose is easier to write. Its absence is detectable and fatal: **a serial with no spoken exchange has no faces, and a serial with no faces has no opposition.**

**Hard gate: at least three spoken dialogue lines per chapter.** If your protagonist cannot speak, give the chapter to someone who can.

## 3.4 The measured house standard

A chapter outside this band **does not read like the fandom** and will be rejected regardless of its facts.

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Sentence average | **≤ 25 words** (the house band is 14–18) |
| Longest sentence | **under 60 words. Zero over 60** |
| Median sentence | **near 11–15 words** |
| Dialogue | **≥ 8–10 spoken lines per 1,000 words**, in any chapter with people in it |
| Chapter length | **2,400–3,000 words.** Up to 3,400 only for a chapter carrying a season's turn |
| Open | **inside a scene** — a body doing something, or a voice speaking |
| Close | **on a hook** — a decision, an arrival, a threat, a name |

### The voice bar — sentence-level DNA

Measured per 1,000 words of prose body (apparatus excluded):

| Marker | House bar |
|---|---|
| **negation** (*did not / was not / had not*) | **17.3** |
| **remembered / forgot** (body and object memory) | **3.8** |
| **"the way X does Y when Z" / "as if"** | **5.1** |
| **noun-phrase fragments** (*"Pockets. Cheap."*) | **5.1** |
| **exclamation marks** | **0** |
| **question marks** | **0** — interrogatives take a full stop |
| **em dashes** | **1.3** — above ~5 means you are leaning on the dash instead of the sentence |
| **plain `said`** as speech tag | **14.3** — tags are boring on purpose |

### The signature construction

> **`[body part or object]` + `[remembered / forgot / found]` + `[the thing it is still holding]`**

- *"Oscar's stomach remembered it had a job."*
- *"The wood under Yin Shu's boots remembered a different weight."*
- *"Dashi's feet forgot they had a job."*
- *"Flender's mouth found something that had not been there when a prince had said lousy."*

**Used for reaction, never for exposition.** It puts memory and attention into **bodies and objects instead of the protagonist's head** — which is how you get interiority without interior monologue.

## 3.5 The five laws that police prose

### The Language Law
**English only. No characters in a script the reader cannot read.** This is **not** a blanket ban on non-ASCII — em dashes, curly quotes and the `◆` marker are correct typography and explicitly allowed.

> An over-broad rule gets quietly ignored, **and a rule that is ignored stops being a rule.**

Names are written in the source language's romanisation, never in characters. Never write the literal two-character escape sequence for a newline **anywhere — including in rules about it.** Express it in a checker as `chr(92) + 'n'`.

### The Number Law
```
PANELS and LEDGERS  → exact figures permitted
PROSE               → round felt counts, in words, spoken or thought by characters
```
A character thinks *he had lived a long time*. The ledger says 743. **Prose carries no digits at all** — not years, not ranks, not distances. It forces magnitude to be expressed as experience rather than bookkeeping: **the difference between a story and a spreadsheet.**

### The Scope Law
*Author, verbatim:* **"Why you making nonsense by writing nonsenses like it becomes boring, wyrite what needs to write not everything, you can skip"**

1. A beat is written **once**. No paragraph explains a paragraph; no chapter re-tells its own events at the close
2. **No overview openings** — no season summaries, no status reports. Open in scene
3. **One line of consequence** after a scene; never a summary of the scene
4. Skip what changes nothing
5. **Cutting removes explanation, never the scene**

### The Plain Language Law
*Author, verbatim:* **"What the hell even this writeing style what you can't write clear that can be understood, why this poem type nonsenses"**

1. Narration says what happened **in plain words.** A stranger must understand every sentence **on one reading**
2. **No invented code nouns as narration.** If a sentence only makes sense to someone who knows your private vocabulary, rewrite it
3. In-world names may appear **only in speech**, and only where the scene explains them
4. The subject of a sentence is **a thing or a body.** Ideas never carry it
5. **A sentence that must be read twice is wrong, no matter how lawful its words**

### The Plain-Scene Law — *the one that arrived last*
*Author, verbatim:* **"Serious mistake complete writeing style is wrong, i need clear and clean, that' can understand and read not some poetry not summery but like how actually written simple."**

> **Real scenes. Simple, clear sentences. No poetry, no summary-chapters.** Time passes in one plain sentence; **the kept moments are written out fully.**

> **This is the hardest law in the guide, and the most important.** The prose laws police words and sentences. **Nothing polices register.** Poetry can be built from lawful words. The pacing law compresses **time**, never **scene**.

### The Canon Voice Law — *the lesson that cost two strikes*
When your target is "how the source is written," **go and read the source.** Fetch it, quote it, derive the rules from the receipts.

Measured difference between source novel voice and literary minimalism:

| The source **explains** | Literary prose **implies** |
|---|---|
| every cause stated openly | causes hidden as texture |
| feelings named flatly | feelings suggested |
| numbers carried freely | numbers avoided |
| the plain subject repeated constantly | pronouns varied |

**Two strikes were spent guessing at a target that was one fetch away.**

## 3.6 Panel discipline

**Slim panel at the top. No footer.**

```
◆ STATUS — Chapter N: <year range>
KIND      : <what the protagonist is, right now>
AGE       : <age>
CLASS     : <rank / tier, and what just opened>
THREAT    : <this chapter's opposition>
BOND      : <only if the chapter turns on a relationship>
LOSS      : <only if something ends>
LAW       : <only if a rule is established or tested>
```

**That is it. Not eleven fields.** Position, canon touched, world changes, wires left open — that is bookkeeping, and it lives in the serial log, where a reader never sees it.

> **A footer in the chapter file is apparatus outgrowing story.**

**Panel economy:** a panel appears **only when there is an update or a gain.** Otherwise it is zero lines. *"Not every time in chapter — you only write when there is update or just gain."*

**The marker law:** `◆` marks apparatus. It belongs to the panel and nowhere else in prose. The one exception is a **book-end card** — a fenced block closing a *book*, opening with `END OF`, **three short lines maximum**, last in the file. **A card that appears every chapter is a footer.**

## 3.7 The twelve style patterns

*Consulted, not stamped whole.* One or two breathing per chapter beats a checklist applied end to end.

| # | Pattern | Deployment rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Question-Title** — titles that are themes, not labels | The title is the chapter's one-sentence answer, unknown until the end |
| 2 | **Arithmetic as Soul** — emotion becomes cost, cost becomes grammar | One price-clause per strong beat. **Never two in a row** |
| 3 | **The Grain-Read** — the speciality expressed as perception | **Extend, never repeat.** Each extension reads a *new class* of thing |
| 4 | **The Drawer** — a motif with a life: literal → figurative → institutional | Once per 8–12 chapters, **evolved one step.** Never name what is in it |
| 5 | **The First Hand In** — love shown as logistics | Show the position **before** the event; let the event arrive |
| 6 | **The Hall That Hears Everything** — institutions as quiet listeners | For consequence beyond the room. **Vary the institution, never the construction twice unchanged** |
| 7 | **The Perspective Panel** — the world seen side-on by strangers who owe the protagonist nothing | One per arc-stretch. **The stranger must gain something from watching** |
| 8 | **Cliff Rhythm** — alternate long and short; grammar mirrors event size | After a long lyrical cliff, take a short one. End on **process** when the threat is offstage |
| 9 | **The Callback-Object Economy** — objects carry history and pay it back | **Every arc spends one old object and banks one new one** |
| 10 | **The Not-Irony Tag** — deadpan humour, never cruel | One per scene. **The tag belongs to the watcher, not the speaker** |
| 11 | **The Refrain, Then the Break** — a short law repeated until breaking it *is* the development | Only **one** refrain live at a time. The break must cost something on-page |
| 12 | **The Ongoing Image** — continuity as weather, water, sound | When the feeling is too large for any character, **give it to the setting** |

**The dialogue dial is a lens, not a default.** The same serial ran lesson-chapters at 44–66% dialogue and hush-chapters at 8–15%. **Choose the chapter's dialogue load like a camera chooses its lens.**

## 3.8 The four things a chapter may never be

- A **thin hinge** that only moves the cast somewhere
- A **copy-machine room** — the same scene type repeated with different nouns
- **Kit-recap or an audit presented as story**
- **Interior meditation with no scene** in it

## 3.9 The filename law

```
ASCII letters, digits, underscores, dots. Nothing else.
No spaces. No special characters. No non-ASCII.
```

`Chapter_07_The_Teeth_in_the_Grass.md` — yes · `Chapter 7 — The Teeth.md` — no

> Upload-safe names survive transfer between agents, platforms and archives. Pretty names get mangled, **and a mangled filename in a handoff is a lost file.**

# PART 4 · CANON DISCIPLINE

## 4.1 The confidence tags

Tag every canon claim. **This is the whole discipline.**

- **`[canon]`** — verified against source material, or multiple independent secondary sources agree
- **`[fan]`** — widely repeated, plausible, **not verified.** Verify before you build a plot on it
- **`[disputed]`** — sources disagree. Pick a reading, **record it, and never switch silently**
- **`[design]`** — your invention. **Must be labelled as yours, never as canon**

> **Never present a `[fan]` or `[design]` claim as `[canon]` — in prose, or in a status file.**

**And know what `[canon]` is worth.** In the house, `[canon]` means *multiple independent secondary sources agree* — **not** *read in the original source text.* That is a weaker guarantee, and you should know it before you build a plot on one of those rows.

## 4.2 The butterfly law — the sharpest form

> **A butterfly effect is not a decoration added on top of a canon scene. It is the reason the scene is different at all. If a canon scene can be dropped into your story unchanged, the butterfly has not been applied — your story is canon with an extra character standing in it.**

- **Effect without its cause shown on-page is not a butterfly. It is a claim**
- **Canon's superlatives must be scoped.** Any line making someone "the strongest/best/highest" in a group your character belongs to **must survive their presence**

> The recorded founding failure: copying canon's *"Xie Xie's soul power is the highest"* into a class that **contained the original character.** That is transcription, and **it erases your character from their own story.**

## 4.3 The seven butterfly categories

Use these instead of random changes. **If a chapter touches canon and none of these fire, ask why not.**

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Presence** | Your character is physically present in a public scene and changes how people react |
| **Absence** | Your character's absence, or another's, changes routine |
| **Relationship** | A friendship or rivalry changes messages, worry, choices, interpretation |
| **Record** | Institutions record your character differently because they exist under that file |
| **Resource** | Money, access and status shape what options are available |
| **Training** | A lesson learned earlier changes an action later |
| **Environmental** | The setting leaves body-level or circumstance-level effects |

> **The `record` category is the most underused and the most powerful.** Institutions write things down. **A report filed in chapter five, read by a clerk in chapter fifteen, acted on in chapter thirty — that is canon-shaped consequence with no combat required.**

## 4.4 The parallel rule

> **Canon runs as itself. Your character lives inside it.**

The world does not centre on them. **If a canon event happens off-page and your character never hears about it, that is correct — and it should stay that way.** The reader can see it in a parallel panel without your character knowing.

**This is the discipline that separates "a story set in that world" from "that world rewritten around me."**

## 4.5 The perspective panel doctrine

1. **Every chapter stages at least one panel belonging to someone who is not your protagonist** — and the panel's scene must be **checked out of canon itself**
2. **A panel with no canon behind it is an invention and must be labelled as one.** A chapter built mostly of such inventions is the wrong chapter
3. **The butterfly test extends to every character**, not just your protagonist
4. **Use everything:** canon scenes, ensemble states, minor viewpoints — an examiner, a kitchen elder, an apprentice, a market vendor, a letter-writer. **The world is full of people who do not know your character exists. Show them**
5. **Locks still govern.** Another character's panel may *approach* a locked door but **never open it**

**Legend accumulates in outside voices.** The protagonist's reputation grows in *other people's* observations, **never in their own narration.**

## 4.6 Knowledge firewalls

> **Standing rule: old trust stays real. Private secrets stay protected. Public consequences must breathe.**

**The four layers**
```
1. HARD TRUTH          — verified canon, universe rules, established on-page facts
2. APPROVED DIVERGENCE — approved changes from source
3. STORY STATE         — everything that has happened on the page
4. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE — what each person knows, believes, misreads, or has never heard
```

**Layer 4 is the one that breaks.** Layers 1–3 are about the world. **Layer 4 is about minds — and minds are not synchronised.**

> **A character never behaves as if they know what they have not learned.**

**Earned by:** being told on-page · witnessing it directly · deducing it from information they actually hold, **with the reasoning shown** · reading it somewhere they have legitimate access.

**Not earned by:** the author needing them to know · the reader knowing it · the character being smart · *"they would obviously have figured it out."*

> **That last one is the trap. Smart characters still cannot know things they have no route to.**

**The breathing rule**
- **Public facts cause public consequences.** If your character's strength is measured officially, the institution records it and people react. **Suppressing that to protect a plot is a failure**
- **Private facts stay private.** They leak when someone *earns* them — never because the plot needs them to

> The tension between those two rules is where most of the good drama lives.

**The second firewall question is as important as the first:** *does any character fail to react to something they definitely know?* **A character who knows a fact and never lets it affect a decision has been written as a prop.**

## 4.7 Power law

### The anti-nerf rule
> **Never reduce your protagonist's power to preserve a canon outcome.**

The reader can feel the author's hand. If your character is publicly stronger than canon expects, **public facts cause public consequences** — paperwork, attention, allocation, eligibility, rivalries.

```
- What does the institution record now?
- Who is threatened by this?
- What resource or opportunity opens or closes?
- Which canon character must now treat them differently?
```
> **If none of those change, you have nerfed by omission.**

### No unearned power — the five questions
```
1. What changed?
2. What caused it?
3. What did they already possess that made this possible?
4. What failed first?
5. What limitation remains?
```
> **Question 4 is the one everyone skips.** A gain that cost nothing and overcame nothing is not progression — it is a gift from the author.

> **Nothing is absolute.** Rank and title labels are *estimates about a fight*, not fixtures. A lower-ranked character with the right counter beats a higher-ranked one — and the reader should be able to see why afterward.

### Measurement parity
- **One measurement source.** One file states current strength. Everything else derives
- **Public and private values may differ — record both.** A beast recorded at 100 years publicly and 480 privately is fine, and it is a plot device. The same beast at 100 in one file and 480 in another **with no explanation** is an error
- **Every number has a source.** Say which — a measurement, an assessment, or a document

### No plot armour
```
- What could be lost here?    If the answer is nothing, the scene has no stakes
- Who could fail?             If nobody can, the scene has no tension
- Why isn't this easy?        If you cannot answer, it should be easy — and over quickly
- What did it cost?           A victory that cost nothing reads as a gift
```

# PART 5 · THE GATES

## 5.1 The principle

> **A chapter is not done when it reads well. It is done when it has been checked.**

And **a check that executes nothing verifies nothing.** If you cannot name the specific function or code path the check actually touched, **you have not checked anything — you have re-read your own draft and felt reassured.**

> **A clean exit code is not a pass when the output is wrong. Read the output.**

## 5.2 The seven hard gates

```
1. Zero characters in an unreadable script — not a blanket non-ASCII ban
2. Zero literal backslash-n sequences
3. Zero digits in prose (panels and ledgers exempt)
4. At least three spoken dialogue lines per chapter
5. Chapter panels contiguous — no overlap, no gap
6. No template or placeholder text left in a shipped file
7. The ◆ marker never appears in prose; at most one book-end card,
   opening with END OF, last in the file
```

**"Mostly passing" is a failure.**

## 5.3 Gate scope — *which gates apply to which files*

> **Getting this wrong produces a wall of false positives that trains everyone to ignore the gate.**

| Gate | Chapters | Templates | Law files | Codex / ledgers | Audit logs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 unreadable script | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| 2 escape sequences | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| 3 digits in prose | yes | — | — | no | no |
| 4 dialogue | yes | — | — | — | — |
| 5 contiguity | yes | — | — | — | — |
| 6 placeholders | **yes** | **NO** | **NO** | yes | **NO** |
| 7 marker discipline | yes | no | no | — | — |

> **Gate 6 must never be applied to templates, law files, or audit logs.** A template with no slot tokens is not a template. A law file that cannot show the panel format cannot teach it. **An audit log that reports `placeholders = 0` is doing its job, not leaking one.**
>
> **Gates 1 and 2 are the only ones that apply everywhere.** They indicate a file is *broken*, rather than a file *being what it is.*

## 5.4 The self-referential false positive

> **A check must not match its own description.**

If a gate fires on the documentation *of the gate*, **the gate is scoped wrong — not the documentation.** Fix the scope, because *"just reword the docs"* **leaves the trap armed for the next file.**

**Recorded instances:** a non-ASCII gate flagging em dashes *in the law explaining the rule* · a placeholder match flagging the word "placeholder" *in a sentence saying "no placeholder text left"* · a placeholder gate flagging *every slot token in every template* · a placeholder gate flagging a log line that **reported finding none.**

## 5.5 Test the gate itself

**Run the selftest after every edit to the checker.** It builds throwaway chapters, each carrying exactly one known defect, and confirms every one is caught — then builds a clean chapter and confirms it passes.

> **Why this is not optional:** the verifier was once run against a proven 33,000-word serial and reported **15 failures. All 15 were false positives. The serial was correct and the gate was wrong** — and nothing revealed that except a hand-built negative test.
>
> **A gate that has only ever been seen to pass is a gate nobody has tested.**

**If you add an eighth gate, add an eighth fixture.** A gate with no fixture is a gate you are hoping works.

## 5.6 The parser pattern that matters

```
WRONG — joining everything after the first fence reclassifies any LATER
fenced block as prose, so the marker gate and the digits gate both fire
on apparatus they should ignore.

RIGHT — every fenced block is apparatus. Prose is only what sits outside
all of them: the even indices past the first fence.
```

> **The wrong version passes every test you think to write — until the day a chapter grows a second fenced block.** Then it produces failures that *look like real defects in the prose.*
>
> **A verification script with a bug in it is worse than no script, because it produces false confidence.**

## 5.7 The honesty rule

> **If the real check is blocked, unblocking it is part of the task.**

If it genuinely cannot be run, **say so plainly in the same breath as the change** — what you did, what you could not check, and why.

> **Presenting an unverified change as done is the failure to avoid. A guess written in the same voice as a verified fact is how you end up being wrong twice.**

## 5.8 The five mis-measurements

*Five things once logged as "missing" that were already present:*

| Claimed missing | Reality |
|---|---|
| numbers were "footer-only" | they were spelled out in prose |
| the strength table was present | it was in headers only |
| a whole power system was "unused" | present in 9 chapters |
| a character's entrance was "missing" | the fight was there; only the arrival was absent |
| a growth mechanic was "never applied" | fully present, and used as a plot engine |

> **The single cause: searching for the words you expect instead of looking for the thing.**

**Countermeasures, all mandatory**
1. **Scan for the concept, not one keyword.** Three phrasings minimum
2. **Check prose, headers and footers separately**
3. **Numbers may be spelled out.** Grep `two hundred and forty-three` as well as `243`
4. **Before logging a defect, try to disprove it.** Spend one call proving yourself wrong
5. **A checker can prove false presence. It cannot prove false absence. Only re-reading can**

## 5.9 Calibrate a threshold before you obey it

> **A check that cries wolf is worse than no check.**

When a threshold produces false positives on correct work, **the threshold is the defect.** Fix the threshold — **do not "fix" the prose.**

# PART 6 · THE CHAPTER LOOP

## 6.1 The loop

```
pre-chapter board  →  draft  →  self-audit  →  gate  →  sync every file  →  ship
```

**"Update everything, every time."**

## 6.2 Set the panel endpoints FIRST

> **Never the reverse.**

This is the highest-value rule in the guide, and it exists because of the most expensive class of error the house ever recorded.

**The procedure**
```
1. Open the continuity ledger and read the anchor table
2. Decide the chapter's start and end dates. Write them in the panel
3. Check the end date against every canon constraint that bounds this story
4. Only then write the chapter, deriving every in-chapter date from the endpoints
5. After drafting, re-derive every date in the prose and check it against the panel
6. Add the new anchors to the table
```

> **Why:** writing dates as prose and reconciling afterwards is how a finished serial shipped **seventeen arithmetic errors and one hard canon violation** — the finale landed **49 years after a canon character had already left the story**, which made the ending impossible as written.
>
> **Not one of those errors was hard to fix. All of them were invisible from inside the draft.** That is exactly why the order matters.

**The anchor table** — one row per dated event, and **carry the arithmetic in the row** so the error becomes visible the moment you type it:
```
| Anchor        | Year | Chapter | Note       |
|---------------|------|---------|------------|
| Mantis arrives|   61 |       3 |            |
| The killing   |   98 |       3 | founds the mercy law |
| Mantis dies   |  104 |       3 | 61 + 43    |
```

**Forward references are promises.** Every *"X years later"* and *"she would not learn until"* gets tracked. **Compute them** — *"in forty-seven years"* from year 753 must land on a year that means something. And **pay them off or cut them.**

## 6.3 The five audits

Run all five, **in this order**, on every chapter.

| Audit | The question |
|---|---|
| **Canon** | **What changed *because of our divergence*?** Anything not deliberately changed remains canon. **An accidental change is a bug, not a liberty** |
| **Continuity** | Ages, dates, injuries, promises, relationships, impossible travel, resources |
| **Character** | Does the voice, the values and the decision-making still belong to this person? **Change must be earned** |
| **Power** | Did strength come from an established cause? What counters remain? Does the world react to what became public? Has a reserved power been granted early? |
| **Tension** | What can be lost? Who can fail? What stays unknown? Why isn't this easy? **If all four answers are "nothing," the chapter has no stakes and should not ship** |

## 6.4 When the audit finds something

> **Fix it, then run the audit again. Not "note it and move on."**

**Check the blast radius.** One wrong date usually means the arithmetic around it is wrong too — the case that produced seventeen errors had a character living 43 years in one line and being alive 37 years later in another. **Fixing the first line without the second just moved the contradiction.**

> **When a class of error appears, sweep for the whole class.** Do not fix the instance you happened to notice.

## 6.5 Record the audit

Every non-trivial audit gets a dated record in `audits/` — **what was found, what was wrong, what was changed, what was verified afterward, and what remains open.**

> **This is how a project survives a handoff. Someone who can read what was already caught does not re-litigate it.**

## 6.6 The ship law

> **Mechanical 80% + authored 20%. Exit 0 only when every automated step is verified.**

- **The mechanical half** — the gate runs, zero failures, every file synced
- **The authored half** — the turn, the registers, the honesty check, the thing only a human can answer

**Exit 0 is claimed only when the automated half is genuinely green.**

# PART 7 · STRUCTURE AND HANDOFF

## 7.1 The file tree

```
your_project/
├── README.md                    ← authority order, file map, live edge
├── foundation/
│   ├── STATUS_PANEL.md          ← THE single status source
│   ├── CONTINUITY.md            ← THE single continuity ledger
│   ├── NO_MISTAKE_LIVE_RULES.md ← the locks, current after chapter N
│   ├── CANON_NOTES.md           ← receipts and confidence tags
│   ├── CANON_LEDGER.md          ← every canon beat touched, and how
│   └── SERIAL_LOG.md            ← per-chapter record
├── codex/
│   ├── CHARACTERS.md
│   ├── TIMELINE.md
│   ├── PLACES.md
│   └── KNOWLEDGE_FIREWALLS.md
├── chapters/
│   └── Chapter_01_....md
└── audits/
    └── <date>_<subject>.md
```

**Do not invent your own layout.** The layout is what lets a fresh reader orient in one read.

## 7.2 What good setup looks like

**A finished setup is about 2,000 words across four files. It should take an afternoon.**

> **The failure mode is the opposite: 25,000 words of world bible before chapter one exists. That is procrastination with a file extension.**
>
> **Nothing enters the codex until it happens in a chapter. A codex full of things that never appear is a lie you maintain at your own expense.**

## 7.3 The handoff file

One file at the project root, named `HANDOFF.md`. It answers, **in order**:

```
1. WHAT THIS IS            — one paragraph. Era, protagonist, premise
2. THE LIVE EDGE           — the last chapter that counts, by number and title
3. THE STATUS SOURCE       — which file is authoritative. Name it
4. THE LOCKS               — the absolutes, verbatim
5. WHAT IS FORBIDDEN NOW   — powers, reveals and events reserved for later
6. WHAT IS STALE           — superseded drafts, and where the live ones are
7. OPEN THREADS            — what is unresolved, and what each one needs
8. WHAT NOT TO RE-LITIGATE — decisions already made, with the reason
9. VERIFICATION            — the command to run, and what passing looks like
```

> **Keep it under 1,500 words. A handoff so long nobody reads it is worse than none.**

**Include:** the locks **verbatim** — *paraphrases drift* · the exact verification command · the errors already caught, **so nobody rediscovers them** · anything the author ruled on, with the date.

**Do not include:** a plot summary · world lore · your reasoning process · **anything you are unsure about, unmarked.**

## 7.4 The stale-file problem

> **A handoff is the highest-risk moment in a long story. Every error that was fixed can be reintroduced by one reader opening one stale file.**

**Three defences**
1. **Stamp them.** An unmistakable banner at the top of every superseded file
2. **Say where the live ones are.** Not just "the old ones are wrong" — **name the directory that is current**
3. **Run the check first.** Before writing anything, run the verification over the whole project and read the output. **Expect failures. Fix them before adding.**

**Classify before stamping.** A blanket stamp over files containing still-binding canon law **destroys valid reference:**
- **State mirrors** (status panels, timelines, dossiers) → stamp superseded
- **Mixed law + state** (world bibles, doctrine files) → **qualified banner**: law binds, state rows are stale
- **Pure law** (canon receipts, prose law, research rules) → **leave alone**

> **An unstamped stale file is a trap with a five-minute fuse.**

## 7.5 The two-copies law

> **A fact maintained in two places will be wrong in one of them.** This is the root cause of nearly every recurring defect in this house.

1. **One authority per fact.** Every fact has exactly one authoritative file
2. **Nothing that can be derived may be typed.** If it can be computed, compute it
3. **Pin the count, or don't state it.** A number in prose must be re-measured when the thing it counts changes — **or stated timelessly**
4. **When two copies disagree, the receipt wins**

> **The parser corollary:** derived documents are **regenerated, not hand-edited.** If you find yourself editing generated output, **you have found a defect in the generator.**

**Correct at the source.** If the panel says one thing and a codex file says another, **fix the codex file. Do not add a note explaining the difference.** Notes explaining contradictions accumulate until the project has more reconciliation text than story.

## 7.6 The author's words are the top layer

When the author rules on something, **record it verbatim, with the date.**

```
Ruling (2026-09-08): Blue Silver Valley IS canon Soul Land 1.
Never "[design]". Do not re-add revoked wording.
```

> **Paraphrased rulings get re-argued. Verbatim rulings with dates do not.**

**And when a ruling is revoked, record the revocation with its own date.** Otherwise someone finds the original ruling, believes it, **and undoes the correction.**

## 7.7 Cross-project discipline

If you run more than one story in the same fandom, **they will bleed into each other.**

Every project needs its own status source, continuity ledger, lock file, and numbering.

> **Numbers never cross.** Two stories in the same world **share canon; they share nothing else.**

# PART 8 · THE MISTAKE ATLAS — the twelve recurring classes

*Full detail in `THE-MISTAKE-ATLAS.md`. Keep this list open.*

| # | Class | The tell | The cure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Assert-without-verifying** | confident output, no receipt | read before asserting; try to disprove your own claim |
| 2 | **Inventing instead of sourcing** | plausible, canon-shaped, unverified | canon-first; label every invention `[design]` |
| 3 | **Centering the OC** | your character is the exception; canon bends | the Multi-Panel Law; scope canon's superlatives |
| 4 | **Register drift** | lawful words, unreadable page | the Plain-Scene Law; measure, don't eyeball |
| 5 | **Two-copies rot** | two files claim to be current | one authority; derive, don't type |
| 6 | **Checkers that lie** | a gate that cannot fire, or fires on correct work | selftest; scope the gate; calibrate thresholds |
| 7 | **Cleanup that cuts the wrong thing** | a "duplicate" that was unique | byte-verify **before** removal |
| 8 | **Misreading the ask** | solving the wrong problem, well | identify the project from its own contents |
| 9 | **Process and state failure** | lost edits, clobbered files | commit first, then rebase; serialise edits |
| 10 | **Handoff failure** | the map names a file that does not exist | one status source; stamp the stale |
| 11 | **Account and publishing failure** | stale public numbers, missing licence | verify public surfaces like any other copy |
| 12 | **Documentation drift** | docs describe a state that has moved | the Two-Copies Law applies to documentation |

### The three lessons that arrived last

> **The prose laws police words and sentences. Nothing polices register.** Poetry can be built from lawful words. **A sentence that must be read twice is wrong no matter how lawful its words.**

> **When the author says "go see how canon is written," GO SEE.** Fetch the source, quote it, derive the rules from receipts.

> **The author's strikes are the curriculum.** Every standing law in this house was born from a verbatim strike, preserved word-for-word.

# PART 9 · QUICK REFERENCE CARDS

### CARD 1 — The twelve locks
```
 1. ERA            — which era, which years
 2. PROTAGONIST    — who, and explicitly what they are NOT
 3. CANON ENTRY    — the specific canon beats touched (name ≥5)
 4. SPINE          — who wants what from the protagonist, and what they'll do
 5. POWER CEILING  — the strongest they become, and what they never get
 6. IDENTITY       — public name vs true name; what the reveal costs
 7. ABSOLUTES      — what they will never do
 8. CANON IMMUNITY — which canon fates may not be rerouted
 9. MEASUREMENT    — how growth is shown on the page
10. VOICE          — POV, tense, register rules
11. CADENCE        — chapters per pass, words per chapter, when audits run
12. HANDOFF        — what a fresh reader must read first
```

### CARD 2 — Before every chapter
```
1. What canon beat does this touch, and how does it stay true?
2. What is the threat? (Not "tension" — a thing that can hurt someone)
3. Who opposes, and do they have a face and a name?
4. What moves? (rank, skill, bond, resource, knowledge — pick one, name it)
5. What is the turn? (What is different at the end?)
6. Which registers? (≥3 of the five)
7. What are the panel endpoints? (Set these BEFORE writing dates)
```

### CARD 3 — The numbers
```
Sentence avg ≤ 25 (band 14–18)      Max sentence < 60, zero over
Median 11–15                        Dialogue ≥ 8–10 lines / 1,000 words
Chapter 2,400–3,000 (max 3,400)     ≥ 3 spoken dialogue lines minimum
Exclamation marks 0                 Question marks 0
Plain "said" tags (not exclaimed)   Digits in prose: 0
```

### CARD 4 — The seven gates
```
1. No unreadable script       5. Panels contiguous
2. No escape sequences        6. No placeholders in shipped files
3. No digits in prose         7. ◆ only in panels; one book-end card max
4. ≥3 spoken dialogue lines
```

### CARD 5 — The audit, in order
```
CANON → CONTINUITY → CHARACTER → POWER → TENSION
Then: fix, re-run, sweep the whole class, and write the dated record.
```

### CARD 6 — When something is wrong
```
1. Find what it SKIPPED, not what it said
2. Walk the five failures in order
3. More than two true? You are rebuilding, not editing
4. Fix the smallest area; preserve established material
5. Re-run the audit
6. Sweep the whole class of error
7. Record it, dated
```

### CARD 7 — The one-page version

> **Consistency > convenience. Causality > coincidence. Character agency > author control. Earned progression > arbitrary escalation. Consequences > spectacle. Truth > memory.**

**Before you write:** set the twelve locks. Fill the spine sentence. Name five canon beats. Write the panel. **Nothing enters the codex until it happens on a page.**

**Before every chapter:** ask the seven questions. **Set the panel endpoints first, then derive every date.**

**While writing:** ≥3 registers · ≥3 spoken lines · no digits in prose · plain words · open in scene · one line of consequence, never a summary · **write it once.**

**After writing:** five audits in order. Run the gate — **and have tested the gate on known-bad input first.** Fix, re-run, sweep the class, record it.
